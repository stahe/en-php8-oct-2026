    4. Application exercise – versions 1 and 2

    4.1. The problem

    The table above makes it possible to calculate the tax in the simplified case of a taxpayer who has only their salary to declare. As note (1) indicates, the tax calculated in this way is the tax before three mechanisms:

    • the capping of the family quotient, which applies to high incomes;
    • the tax discount (décote) and the tax reduction, which apply to low incomes.

    The tax calculation therefore comprises the following steps [http://impotsurlerevenu.org/comprendre-le-calcul-de-l-impot/1217-calcul-de-l-impot-2019.php]:

    We propose to write a program that calculates a taxpayer's tax in the simplified case of a taxpayer who has only their salary to declare. The rules used are those of the 2019 tax on 2018 income. They will serve as the common thread of the whole document: the algorithm will remain the same from one version of the application to the next; only its architecture will change.

    4.1.1. Calculating the gross tax

    The gross tax can be calculated as follows.

    First, the taxpayer's number of shares is calculated:

    • each parent brings 1 share;
    • the first two children each bring 1/2 share;
    • the following children bring one share each.

    The number of shares is therefore:

    • nbParts=1+nbEnfants*0.5+(nbEnfants-2)*0.5 if the employee is not married;
    • nbParts=2+nbEnfants*0.5+(nbEnfants-2)*0.5 if they are married;

    where nbEnfants is their number of children, the term (nbEnfants-2)*0.5 applying only from 3 children on. Then:

    • the taxable income R=0.9*S is calculated, where S is the annual salary;
    • the family quotient QF=R/nbParts is calculated;
    • the gross tax I is calculated from the following data (2019):

    Each row has 3 fields: field1, field2, field3. To calculate the tax I, we look for the first row where QF<=field1 and take the values of that row. For example, for a married employee with two children and an annual salary S of 50000 euros:

    • taxable income: R=0.9*S=45000;
    • number of shares: nbParts=2+2*0.5=3;
    • family quotient: QF=45000/3=15000.

    The 1st row where QF<=field1 is the following:

    1

    The tax I is then equal to 0.14*R – 1394.96*nbParts=[0.14*45000-1394.96*3]=2115. The tax is rounded down to the euro.

    If the relation QF<=field1 holds as early as the 1st row, then the tax is zero.

    If QF is such that the relation QF<=field1 never holds, then the coefficients of the last row are used. Here:

    1

    which gives the gross tax I=0.45*R – 20163.45*nbParts.

    4.1.2. Capping of the family quotient

    To find out whether the capping of the family quotient QF applies, the gross tax is recalculated without the children. Again for the married employee with two children and an annual salary S of 50000 euros:

    • taxable income: R=0.9*S=45000;
    • number of shares: nbParts=2 (the children are no longer counted);
    • family quotient: QF=45000/2=22500.

    The 1st row where QF<=field1 is the following:

    1
    27519    0.14    1394.96

    The tax I is then equal to 0.14*R – 1394.96*nbParts=[0.14*45000-1394.96*2]=3510.

    • maximum benefit related to the children: 1551 * 2 = 3102 euros;
    • minimum tax: 3510-3102 = 408 euros.

    The gross tax with 3 shares already calculated, 2115 euros, is greater than the minimum tax of 408 euros, so the family capping does not apply here.

    In general, the gross tax is sup(impôt1, impôt2) where:

    • [impôt1]: is the gross tax calculated with the children;
    • [impôt2]: is the gross tax calculated without the children and reduced by the maximum benefit (here 1551 euros per half-share) related to the children.

    4.1.3. Calculating the tax discount

    Again for the married employee with two children and an annual salary S of 50000 euros:

    The gross tax (2115) resulting from the previous step is less than 2627 euros for a couple (1595 euros for a single person): the tax discount therefore applies. It is obtained with the following calculation:

    • discount = threshold (couple=1970 / single=1196) - 0.75 * gross tax;
    • discount = 1970-0.75*2115 = 383.75 rounded to 384 euros;
    • new gross tax = 2115-384 = 1731 euros.

    4.1.4. Calculating the tax reduction

    Below a certain threshold, a 20% reduction is applied to the gross tax resulting from the previous calculations. In 2019, the thresholds are as follows:

    • single: 21037 euros;
    • couple: 42074 euros (the figure 37968 used in the example above seems wrong).

    This threshold is increased by the value: 3797 * (number of half-shares brought by the children).

    Again for the married employee with two children and an annual salary S of 50000 euros:

    • their taxable income (45000 euros) is below the threshold (42074+2*3797)=49668 euros;
    • they are therefore entitled to a 20% reduction of their tax: 1731 * 0.2 = 346.2 euros rounded to 347 euros;
    • the taxpayer's gross tax becomes: 1731-347 = 1384 euros.

    4.1.5. Calculating the net tax

    Our calculation will stop there: the net tax to be paid will be 1384 euros. In reality, the taxpayer may benefit from other reductions, in particular for donations to public-interest or general-interest organizations.

    4.1.6. The case of high incomes

    Our previous example corresponds to the majority of employee cases. However, the tax calculation is different for high incomes.

    4.1.6.1. Capping of the 10% reduction on annual income

    In most cases, the taxable income is obtained by the formula: R=0.9*S where S is the annual salary. This is called the 10% reduction. This reduction is capped. In 2019:

    • it cannot be greater than 12502 euros;
    • it cannot be less than 437 euros.

    Let us take the case of an unmarried employee with no children and an annual salary of 200000 euros:

    • the 10% reduction is 20000 euros > 12502 euros. It is therefore brought down to 12502 euros.

    4.1.6.2. Capping of the family quotient

    Let us take a case where the family capping presented in the section "Capping of the family quotient" above applies: a couple with three children and an annual income of 100000 euros. Let us go through the calculation steps again:

    • the 10% allowance is 10000 euros < 12502 euros. The taxable income R is therefore 100000-10000=90000 euros;
    • the couple has nbParts=2+0.5*2+1=4 shares;
    • its family quotient is therefore QF=R/nbParts=90000/4=22500 euros;
    • its gross tax I1 with children is I1=0.14*90000-1394.96*4=7020 euros;
    • its gross tax I2 without children:
      • QF=90000/2=45000 euros;
      • I2=0.3*90000-5798*2=15404 euros;
      • the family quotient capping rule says that the benefit brought by the children cannot exceed (1551*4 half-shares)=6204 euros. Here, however, it is I2-I1=15404-7020=8384 euros, therefore greater than 6204 euros;
      • the gross tax is therefore recalculated as I3=I2-6204=15404-6204=9200 euros.

    This couple will have neither a discount nor a reduction, and its final tax will be 9200 euros.

function cutNewLineChar(string $ligne): string
{
    // on supprime la marque de fin de ligne de $ligne si elle existe
    // (\r\n sous Windows, \n sous Unix)
    return rtrim($ligne, "\r\n");
}

    // calcul de l'impôt
// --------------------------------------------------------------------------
function calculImpot(string $marié, int $enfants, float $salaire, array $limites, array $coeffR, array $coeffN): array
{
    // $marié : oui, non
    // $enfants : nombre d'enfants
    // $salaire : salaire annuel
    // $limites, $coeffR, $coeffN : les tableaux des données permettant le calcul de l'impôt
    //
    // calcul de l'impôt avec enfants
    $result1 = calculImpot2($marié, $enfants, $salaire, $limites, $coeffR, $coeffN);
    $impot1 = $result1["impôt"];
    // calcul de l'impôt sans les enfants
    if ($enfants !== 0) {
        $result2 = calculImpot2($marié, 0, $salaire, $limites, $coeffR, $coeffN);
        $impot2 = $result2["impôt"];
        // application du plafonnement du quotient familial
        if ($enfants < 3) {
            // PLAFOND_QF_DEMI_PART euros pour les 2 premiers enfants
            $impot2 = $impot2 - $enfants * PLAFOND_QF_DEMI_PART;
        } else {
            // PLAFOND_QF_DEMI_PART euros pour les 2 premiers enfants, le double pour les suivants
            $impot2 = $impot2 - 2 * PLAFOND_QF_DEMI_PART - ($enfants - 2) * 2 * PLAFOND_QF_DEMI_PART;
        }
    } else {
        $impot2 = $impot1;
        $result2 = $result1;
    }
    // on prend l'impôt le plus fort avec le taux et la surcôte qui vont avec
    if ($impot1 > $impot2) {
        $impot = $impot1;
        $taux = $result1["taux"];
        $surcôte = $result1["surcôte"];
    } else {
        $surcôte = $impot2 - $impot1 + $result2["surcôte"];
        $impot = $impot2;
        $taux = $result2["taux"];
    }
    // calcul d'une éventuelle décôte
    $décôte = getDecote($marié, $salaire, $impot);
    $impot -= $décôte;
    // calcul d'une éventuelle réduction d'impôts
    $réduction = getRéduction($marié, $salaire, $enfants, $impot);
    $impot -= $réduction;
    // résultat
    return [
        "impôt" => (int) floor($impot),
        "surcôte" => (int) $surcôte,
        "décôte" => (int) $décôte,
        "réduction" => (int) $réduction,
        "taux" => $taux,
    ];
}

function calculImpot2(string $marié, int $enfants, float $salaire, array $limites, array $coeffR, array $coeffN): array
{
    // $marié : oui, non
    // $enfants : nombre d'enfants
    // $salaire : salaire annuel
    // $limites, $coeffR, $coeffN : les tableaux des données permettant le calcul de l'impôt
    //
    // nombre de parts
    if ($marié === "oui") {
        $nbParts = $enfants / 2 + 2;
    } else {
        $nbParts = $enfants / 2 + 1;
    }
    // 1 part par enfant à partir du 3e
    if ($enfants >= 3) {
        // une demi-part de + pour chaque enfant à partir du 3e
        $nbParts += 0.5 * ($enfants - 2);
    }
    // revenu imposable
    $revenuImposable = getRevenuImposable($salaire);
    // surcôte
    $surcôte = floor($revenuImposable - 0.9 * $salaire);
    // pour des pbs d'arrondi
    if ($surcôte < 0) {
        $surcôte = 0;
    }
    // quotient familial
    $quotient = $revenuImposable / $nbParts;
    // est mis à la fin du tableau limites pour arrêter la boucle qui suit
    $limites[count($limites) - 1] = $quotient;
    // calcul de l'impôt
    $i = 0;
    while ($quotient > $limites[$i]) {
        $i++;
    }
    // du fait qu'on a placé $quotient à la fin du tableau $limites, la boucle précédente
    // ne peut déborder du tableau $limites
    // maintenant on peut calculer l'impôt
    $impôt = floor($revenuImposable * $coeffR[$i] - $nbParts * $coeffN[$i]);
    // résultat
    return ["impôt" => $impôt, "surcôte" => $surcôte, "taux" => $coeffR[$i]];
}

    4.4.2. Conclusion

    Version 2 is much more flexible than version 1. If, in another year, the tax calculation algorithm remains the same, only the tax brackets and the calculation constants change. It is then enough to update the file [taxadmindata.json]. With version 1, you have to go into the code to modify the tax brackets and the calculation constants. Yet the people who have to change the values of the tax brackets and calculation constants probably do not have access to the algorithm's code.

    Version 2 nevertheless has a flaw: the array [$taxAdminData] must be passed to all the functions, and nothing guarantees that it contains all the expected keys. A typo in a key of the JSON file would only be detected at calculation time. Version 3 will store these data in an object of a class [TaxAdminData] whose properties are declared and typed.
